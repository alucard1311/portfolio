Ergos: A Developmental Ladder to a Governed Robot Mind

Vinay Naidu · 2026 · research thesis · simulation study (LIBERO)

Robots today are manufactured — finished at training time, frozen at the age of their dataset. This document reports a program that raises one instead: a serial ladder of kill-gated experiments, each with bars frozen before the run, that ends with a governed memory grafted onto a respected frozen robot body. Failures are published alongside wins; every number is a live-recorded receipt.

Abstract

Vision-language-action models have competent hands and frozen minds: they cannot be taught a new word at deployment, remember nothing across a restart, cannot provably forget, and guess rather than admit ignorance. We report a developmental program — the B-ladder — that builds the missing faculties as separate, testable organs and holds them to pre-registered, kill-gated bars judged only by the benchmark's own success checker or independent oracles, never by the organs themselves. A from-scratch small body established the substrate: visual symbols that stay glued to objects through manipulation (15/15 gates, three seeds), a motion algebra whose exact inverse detects tampered motion logs at AUROC 1.0000, and a spinal cord in which memory reads are provably load-bearing — shuffling them collapses behavior 1.9–2.2× — and in which honest refusal required its own dedicated organ. The program then hit two instructive walls, both published: a carry wall (every closed-loop failure traced to the hands, never the memory) and an occlusion law (at the moment of placement the arm blocks every available camera, capping visual self-assessment), including a pre-registered gate that failed its own frozen bar by a single row and stands as a FAIL. Finally, the governed store was grafted onto a frozen OpenVLA-7B: taught a new goal in one typed sentence, it drove full closed-loop carries at 29/30, persisted across a complete model restart 10/10 (twin: 0/10), deleted on command with a hash-verified certificate whose forgery probe was caught, and refused instructions it was never taught 10/10 with zero false refusals. The base model was never trained. The full knowledge lifecycle — teach, act, forget, refuse, re-teach, act — ran end-to-end in 118.6 seconds.

§1 · Motivation

Manufactured vs. raised

A modern robot foundation model is manufactured: its competence is fixed the day training ends. The world's best labs froze the policy deliberately, and their own research supports the decision — writable weights are where regressions, forgetting, and unaccountable behavior come from. Freezing is what made these models dependable enough to ship. But the freeze has four consequences that no amount of scale removes:

  1. You can't teach them. Your home, your words, your objects are not in any dataset — and a frozen policy cannot take a correction without a training run.
  2. They remember nothing about you. Whatever a session establishes is gone when the session ends.
  3. They can't forget. There is no mechanism to remove something they were told — let alone prove it was removed.
  4. They never say "I don't know." Asked for something outside their training, they guess. A guess in a kitchen is broken glass, or worse.

The thesis of this program is that the remedy is not a better training run but a different kind of component: a governed memory that sits between the user and the frozen policy, in which four capabilities exist as structure rather than as tuned behavior. We call them the four owned organs: teach-by-write (a new fact lands by writing a record, not a gradient), no-forget (the base is never trained, so old competence cannot erode), certified-delete (removal emits a verifiable certificate, and forged certificates fail verification), and honest-abstain (when no memory resolves a request, the system refuses to act rather than guessing).

This is governance by structure. An organism that fails by returning nothing traceable is categorically different from one that confidently fabricates: the first can be audited, corrected, and trusted with a household; the second cannot. The experiments below ask whether the four organs can be built, whether they survive contact with a real closed-loop body, and whether the whole assembly can be demonstrated on a body the field already respects — with every claim carrying its receipt.

§2 · Method

The ladder discipline

The program is organized as a ladder: a serial sequence of rungs, each a self-contained experiment with its own question, its own pass bars, and its own kill condition. No rung starts before the prior rung is blessed or its failure is characterized and banked. Five rules make the ladder trustworthy:

  • Bars are frozen before the run. Every rung's pass thresholds and kill conditions are written down before any gated data is collected. After that, a miss is a result, not a bug to patch. Where a bar was ever amended, the amendment was made pre-run and is recorded as such.
  • Anti-circularity. Success is judged only by the benchmark's own success checker or by an independent oracle (for example, the simulator's ground-truth object poses) — never by the organs under test. The memory never grades the memory. The judges are never trained.
  • Every claim carries a live-recorded receipt. Each gated run emits per-episode machine-readable records — outcomes, decisions, timings, certificate fields — and every number in this document traces to one. The demonstration film goes further: its build asserts each on-screen value against the source manifests and refuses to render a frame that contains a character not present in the file.
  • Eyeball every test. No aggregate is believed until per-example prediction-vs-ground-truth rows (and, for embodied runs, rendered rollouts) have been inspected. Several findings below — including the program's most important failure diagnoses — came from the eyeball pass, not the metric.
  • Failures are published. Rungs that missed their bars appear in this document with the same prominence as rungs that passed, because a ladder that only reports wins is indistinguishable from one that lowered its bars.

Two standing controls run through everything. The manufactured twin is defined once and reused at every rung: the same body, the same data budget, the same training where training exists, tuned in good faith — the "manufactured" lifecycle against which development is measured. The shuffled-memory control deliberately corrupts the mapping between what the memory resolves and what the body receives; if behavior does not collapse under shuffle, the memory was decorative and any win is disallowed.

The discipline is not free, and it is not decorative either. During the big-body run the adversarial review layer caught two orchestrator-authored errors before either could cost a run: a wrong instruction table that would have silently invalidated all three experimental arms, and an inverted axis in the independent pose oracle. Both were surfaced by a cheap one-task smoke gate run before the expensive full battery — cheap validation before expensive runs is itself one of the ladder's operating laws.

§3 · The small body

B0–B2: symbol, witness, spinal cord

Before any respected body was involved, the faculties were built and gated on a from-scratch small body — deliberately weak hands, so that every claim about memory would have to stand on its own.

B0 — Birth of the symbol

The question. On text, the token is the entity. Embodied, a blob of pixels must become a stable symbol: the same object must map to the same memory slot across frames, through manipulation, and a never-seen object must be recognizably new. If the symbol cannot be born, nothing above it can be trusted, so this rung carried the ladder's first kill condition.

The bars. Five gates, three seeds each, on real benchmark pixels with ground-truth identity from the simulator: cross-frame slot-glue ≥ 0.90; re-address of a known object by type and by instance ≥ 0.85; calibrated abstention on never-written objects, AUROC ≥ 0.85; and no fragmentation or collapse of identities (clustering agreement ≥ 0.80).

The result: 15/15 — every gate, every seed.

Two findings mattered beyond the pass. First, a feature-conditioning lever that had been decisive on the program's language-substrate work hurt on frozen vision features in every seed — levers do not cargo-cult across substrates, and each graft must re-earn its recipe. Second, the residual errors were not the look-alike twin objects the benchmark is built around; they were occlusion and motion — a plate reduced to a crescent by the bowl placed on it, a grasped object half-hidden behind the gripper. The identity of the acted-on object has to come from continuous tracking, not from a static write-time snapshot. That observation became a design input two rungs later, and its larger cousin returns as a named wall in §4.

B1 — Motors as witness

The question. Does an exact algebra of rigid motions earn its place in the body, against ordinary learned baselines, on real robot motion streams?

The result: a partial, published as such. As a predictor of future motion the exact representation tied the simplest baseline — on smooth teleoperation streams the output parameterization of a learned motion head is a wash, and at long horizons prediction noise swamps everything. That gate passed on only one of three seeds, and the miss is reported. But as an algebra the organ was redeemed twice over: its exact inverse detects corrupted motion logs — a spliced-in segment from another demonstration, or a 5° perturbation — at AUROC 1.0000 on every seed (n = 400 per seed), and its composed error stays flat at 1.6e-4 degrees from 5-step to 50-step chains while naive integration grows 0.025 → 1.008, a 40× drift.

Banked law — B1

The motor organ's real value on real robot streams is the algebra, not the regression target: an exact inverse (a perfect tamper and splice witness) and exact drift-free composition. Motors redeem as a spine and a witness, not a crystal ball.

The splice witness deserves a note: it is the primitive of a deposable robot — a machine whose motion log can be checked for fabrication algebraically, by anyone, after the fact. It sits beside the deletion certificate in the same family of accountability structures.

B2 — The spinal cord

The question. Can the composite work: an instruction resolves to a memory slot, the slot's content conditions the motor output, and the whole chain earns its keep against the manufactured twin?

The first run: 5/9 — with one gate failed outright. The load-bearing test passed decisively on all three seeds: shuffling the address-to-slot mapping collapsed rollout quality and drove grip accuracy from 0.93–0.94 down to 0.59–0.69. The memory read was doing real work. Parity with the twin passed on two of three seeds. But the abstention gate — refuse instructions naming objects that were never written — failed 2/10, 1/10, 0/10. The diagnosis, from the per-example eyeball pass: on a training vocabulary saturated by a single object type, the address's confidence signal carries no novelty information at all; absent-object and present-object confidence distributions overlapped completely.

The redemption: 9/9 on three fresh seeds. The fix was not a threshold — it was a new organ: a small verifier trained only to check whether a name and a seen object align, with the probe vocabulary fenced out of its training by construction. With the verifier in place, the absent and present score distributions never cross on any seed, abstention passed 10/10 on every seed with false-abstain rates of 0.08, 0.00, and 0.02, and the old threshold rule — re-run inside the same experiment as a control — still failed. The parity gate also cleared on all three seeds at an equalized training budget, with one seed strictly beating the twin; the shuffled control collapsed 1.92–2.24×.

Banked law — B2

Instruction-level abstention does not come free from the addressing machinery — it needs its own organ. Verify-don't-match: a verifier trained to check alignment separates absent from present with a gap that confidence thresholds never see.

A second law rode along: short-horizon open-loop imitation error is saturated by motion priors — it cannot arbitrate whether vision is helping. Only the closed loop can. That law priced the next rung correctly and set up the first wall.

§4 · The walls

Two named walls, both published

A developmental program earns trust by what it does when the ladder stops going up. The B-ladder hit two walls. Both were characterized, named as laws, and published; one includes a pre-registered test that failed its own bar by a single row and stands as a FAIL. This section is the credibility centerpiece of the document.

4.1 — The carry wall

Dropped into the live closed-loop benchmark, the blessed small-body spinal cord scored near zero — and so did its manufactured twin, at the same floor. What followed was a four-stage diagnostic arc that solved, in order and each with a causal receipt, every organ except one.

  • Grasp — solved. The initial failure mode (reach without ever closing the gripper) was eliminated: grippers closed in 87–100% of episodes across arms and seeds. And the closed loop finally separated vision from blindness: the organism succeeded 12/300 against the shuffled control's 0/300 (one-sided Fisher p = 2.2e-4) — memory reads are load-bearing even at a 4% success floor.
  • Goal address — solved. The original addressing machinery scored 0.000 at addressing a goal noun it was never trained on, while the B2 alignment verifier addressed the same noun at 1.000, seed-invariant — the fourth independent win for the verify-don't-match law. With it, goal addressing ran at ~1.0 through full episodes.
  • Placement — solved. With the goal coming from memory, releases landed 1.5–4.5 cm from the believed goal (shuffled control: 20–25 cm — causal proof the read drives the release) while the belief itself was 8–46 cm off in the world frame. Rather than repair the coordinate system, the fix was to close the perceptual loop around the read at the moment of action. The arc's proudest receipt: one rollout landed the object 6.6 mm from the true goal while the system's own belief was 87 mm off — a 13× decoupling; the eyes absorbed a calibration bias the coordinate frame never fixed. Honesty at the act came with it: 0 blind releases, 199 honest aborts, 7 justified perception-gate overrides, and 41 of 210 retries recovered.
  • The carry — not solved. Address at 1.0, servo converging, release honest — and roughly 90% of episodes still died holding the object in transit. Grasp security through transport was the single unsolved organ, upstream of everything that worked.
Banked law — the carry wall

Every bless failure in the small body's closed-loop arc traced to the hands, never the memory. The governed mind was ready before its body was.

One more finding from this arc is uncomfortable and therefore worth stating plainly: at equal training budget, the composed honest pipeline lost the raw percentage-point race to the fused twin (pooled 0.033 vs 0.123 at the final stage). Part of that gap is an honesty tax — trajectories the twin converts into lucky fumbles-onto-the-target, the organism's machinery converts into structured aborts. Twin-relative gates on absolute success reward fused luck; the organism's advantage is measurable in mechanism receipts — shuffle collapse, statistical separation from blindness, truth-vs-belief decoupling — and that is where this program stakes its claims.

4.2 — The occlusion law, and the pre-registration that failed by one row

For a system to be raised rather than manufactured, it must eventually grade its own attempts — otherwise every lesson needs a human or an oracle. A study on the small body asked whether the organism could self-assess success from what it sees. The answer was a law:

Banked law — occlusion

Vision-based self-assessment capped near 0.87 for a structural reason: at the moment of placement, the arm occludes every available camera — overhead and wrist alike, because the wrist camera rides the occluding arm. At the one instant that decides success, the body is standing in front of its own eyes.

The fix direction follows from the law: if vision is structurally blind at the decisive moment, self-assessment must lean on non-visual effort and metacognition signals — stall duration, wall-clock, the trend of goal-confidence over the attempt. A gate built on such signals and calibrated on one seed looked excellent: 13/14 recovered at 1.000 precision.

Then the discipline was applied. A pre-registered test froze the bars and ran the gate on a held-out seed it had never seen: 100 fresh episodes containing 12 true successes.

8 / 12recovered — bar ≥ 7 ✓
0.889precision (8/9) — bar 0.90 ✗
FAILverdict, per pre-registration

The gate cleared its recovery bar and missed its frozen precision bar by exactly one row — one false positive where the pre-registration allowed, in effect, none. The verdict stands as a FAIL. It gets no asterisk. More telling still: a pre-declared, report-only alternative rule (wall-clock ≤ 80 s) happened to go 8 true positives, 0 false positives — a perfect 1.000 on this very test set. It was not promoted, because promoting a rule after seeing the held-out data is precisely the move the discipline exists to forbid. The held-out seed is now burned — it has been seen, and can never again serve as a frozen test. A fresh pre-registration on a new seed is the only honest path forward, and that is the recorded plan.

This episode is presented as a worked example rather than an embarrassment. A program that cannot show you a pre-registration failing by one row — and standing — cannot ask you to believe its passes. The bars in §5 are credible because the bar here was allowed to draw blood.

§5 · The big-body graft

BB0–BB2: the governed store on a respected body

The carry wall defined the graft. The field has bodies with 70–90% benchmark motors and frozen, unteachable minds; the B-ladder had a governed, teachable mind on weak hands. The BB rungs join them: the governed store, unchanged in kind, grafted onto a frozen OpenVLA-7B — a body the field respects — with the base model never trained. Every experiment runs the same body with two minds: ERGOS (the governed memory) and TWIN (the untouched original, given raw text).

BB0 — Baseline: is the body honestly wired?

Before any organ touched it, the frozen body was run on the full benchmark suite: 10 tasks × 20 trials. Pooled success: 166/200 = 83.0%, against a published figure of ~84.7% — within noise, so the harness reproduces the body's known competence rather than a degraded copy of it. BB2 — Teach-through-read: the rung the program exists for

In BB2 the instruction channel is fed from memory. A user teaches in one typed sentence; the store writes a record; at inference, the store resolves the user's request and composes the natural-language instruction the frozen policy receives. If no memory resolves the request, a verifier-gated abstain stops the rollout before it starts. Delete removes the record, emits a certificate, and behavior reverts. The policy itself is untouched throughout — the governed store stands between user and body. Three teach modalities were run, 10 carries per target:

What each arm proves. Alias teaches an arbitrary new word: "snackbowl" is bound to a particular bowl in one sentence, and "pick up the snackbowl" then drives a full closed-loop fetch, 10/10 on the taught target. The twin, handed the same word, flails to its 220-step budget — the word means nothing to it. Refer teaches a descriptive binding ("my usual bowl") with the same result; its differentiator is persistence, below. Pref teaches a standing rule — "when there are two bowls, always the left one" — that then governs an ambiguous request. The twin, with no rule, hits the intended bowl only by chance: 0/30.

Persistence: the un-fakeable part

The headline receipt is the one no context window can imitate. The entire process was killed. The 7-billion-parameter body was reloaded from scratch in a fresh process; the store was loaded from disk; nothing was re-taught. The taught goal still drove the body 10 out of 10 — and the twin, which has no store, woke empty: 0/10, by construction. Same result on both teach modalities tested. This is knowledge in a durable governed store, not residue in a session.

Certified delete — and a forgery, caught

Deletion is the organ the law cares about. On command, the record is removed and a certificate is emitted whose hash roots any third party can recompute: the store's committed root before deletion, its root after, and a verification verdict. The honest certificate verifies true. Then the auditor was probed with a forgery — a certificate claiming deletion while the data was secretly kept. It fails verification. Caught. After a real delete, the request that used to succeed reverts to honest abstention: 9/9 across the delete battery on each teach arm.

The shuffle control, and the pref caveat

The shuffled-memory control held on the big body exactly as it had on the small one: force the store to resolve to the wrong record and the carry fails (0/5) — it does not silently succeed. A memory whose corruption is visible in behavior is a memory that is actually being used.

The pref arm's 0.733 sits below its 0.80 bar and is reported as the miss it is. It is also localized: 10/10 and 10/10 on two of the three scenes, 2/10 on the third — and the per-rollout oracle rows show the rule selected the correct bowl there while the body fumbled the carry at a placement region where the base policy is weak. The memory picked right; the hand missed. The carry wall of §4.1, met again on a body twenty times the size — which is precisely what a law is for.

§6 · The Soma Cycle

How a lesson survives the night

The persistence result has a deliberately biological framing. During the day, a lesson lands in short-term memory — fast to write, easy to hold while you are talking to the robot. When the robot powers down, the Soma Cycle files the day's short-term memories into long-term memory, the way sleep consolidates what you learned into what you know. At dawn, a full restart finds the lesson already there.

The claim is survival, and only survival. The system does not improve overnight; it does not reorganize itself into something smarter; nothing is claimed beyond what the 10/10 after-restart receipt tests directly — that what was taught in the day is still there, and still drives the body, after the process that learned it has ceased to exist. That framing is stated here explicitly because it is the kind of claim that inflates easily, and this program's rule is that a claim may not exceed its receipt.

§7 · Honest scope

What these results are — and aren't

  • Directional, single-seed. The big-body results are one seed on one workstation GPU, with lean trial counts (10 carries per target). They demonstrate the mechanism. Scored, quotable statistics require the pre-declared multi-seed referee run (§9); until then these numbers are directional and are labeled so everywhere they appear.
  • Simulation. All robot behavior is in the LIBERO benchmark on an OpenVLA-7B body. No physical robot was run.
  • The motor ceiling is the twin's ceiling, by design. Because the store composes the instruction the frozen policy receives, motor success is capped at what the body can do with the composed sentence. The contribution is the governed layer — resolve, persist, delete, abstain — not better hands. The control that proves this is also the honesty of it: typing the composed sentence by hand produces byte-identical behavior. The store adds naming and governance, not skill.
  • Refusal is currently lexical. ERGOS refuses when no memory resolves the words it was given. A visual-grounding check — refusing because the named thing is not seen — is built but was not wired into these runs; no reported abstention is visually verified. That is the next rung, not this one.
  • Certificates are auditable, not signed. The deletion certificate's roots can be recomputed by a third party, and forged certificates fail verification — but in these runs the same process writes the claim and checks it. Cryptographic signing is engineering, not research, and is explicitly future work.
  • The small-body gates had scaffolding. B0 was given ground-truth segmentation (its question was symbol stability, not self-segmentation), and the small body's closed-loop arc never met its absolute success bar — it closed as a characterized partial whose residual, the carry, is named and assigned.
  • Success is judged externally, always. Only the benchmark's own checker or an independent pose oracle ever decided an outcome. Nothing in this program grades itself — and where self-grading was attempted as a research question, its pre-registered test failed and is reported as failed (§4.2).

§8 · Position in the field

The frozen brain, and the unshipped join

Nothing here argues against the frozen brain. The opposite: the field's decision to freeze robot foundation policies was correct, and recent work from the strongest labs independently supports the deeper pattern this program is built on — that gradient flow from action learning can corrupt a base model's knowledge, and that the remedy is to insulate the base and feed content through a separate channel (see, e.g., "Knowledge Insulation," arXiv:2505.23705). That is external, respected validation of the frozen-base-plus-channel split these experiments run on.

What no one ships is the join: a channel that is also governed — writable in one sentence, durable across restarts, deletable with a certificate a third party can check, and honest about its own gaps. Retrieval systems write and read, but do not certify deletion or gate action on resolution failure; frozen policies act, but cannot be taught. The four organs together, demonstrated on a body the field respects, with a twin control and external judges — that combination is the contribution claimed here, and it is deliberately a modest one: a governance layer, not a better robot. One further methodological position: published absolute benchmark numbers in this area are known to be sensitive to evaluation recipe, so this program stakes nothing on absolute scores — every comparison is twin-relative under identical plumbing, which is the defensible form.

§9 · Future work

Three pre-committed directions

BB3 — below the sentence

The prompt channel caps the organism at the twin's motor ceiling. BB3 asks whether memory can condition the body below the sentence — embedding-level conditioning — and wires the already-built visual-grounding check into the abstain path, so that refusal can be about what is seen, not only about what was said. BB3 gets its own pre-registration before its first run, per the ladder rules.

The π0 gauntlet — a second body, bars already frozen

If the governed store is real, it is body-agnostic. A pre-registration — frozen 2026-07-26, before bring-up — commits to grafting the same store, with zero model changes, onto a second respected frozen body: Physical Intelligence's open-sourced π0. The framing is homage — their hands, a governed mind — and the bars are already locked:

Explicitly out of scope: any claim about models that are not open-sourced, and any overnight-improvement claim.

Hardening: seeds, referees, and the burned seed's successor

Three commitments close the list. First, every directional number in §5 goes to a pinned, independent multi-seed referee run before it is ever quoted as a scored statistic. Second, the self-grading gate of §4.2 gets a fresh pre-registration on a new, never-seen seed — the only honest path left after the last one was burned. Third, the carry wall folds into the development-curve rung the ladder was always pointed at: the organism's honest aborts are exactly the experience stream a raised system should learn from, and the success-vs-experience curve — the organism climbing where the frozen twin stays flat — remains the figure this program exists to earn.
